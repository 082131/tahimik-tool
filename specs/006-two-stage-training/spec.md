## Plain-language summary

The model learns in two rounds. First it practices on a large pile of fake messy
sentences the computer generated by scrambling clean text — cheap, unlimited,
and you always know the right answer because you made the mess yourself. Then it
practices on a smaller pile of real messy sentences that human annotators
cleaned by hand.

Fake data teaches the general shape of Filipino noise. Real data calibrates it
to how people actually write. Doing both is what the literature says works.

**Critically, all three model variants run this identical pipeline.** That is
what makes the final comparison fair — same data, same splits, same schedule,
same hardware, so any difference in results traces to the compression method
and nothing else.

## What the manuscript specifies

> Control variables held constant across all three models: "the training data,
> the train/validation/test partitioning, the model hyperparameters, and the
> hardware used for training and evaluation."

Synthetic pretraining is justified from the literature:

> "Samuel and Straka (2021), whose intermediate synthetic pretraining step
> produced a substantial gain in error reduction rate from 59.2% to 64.8%,
> demonstrating that synthetic noise pretraining is an effective training
> strategy for ByT5 normalization."

Synthetic corruption follows Tagalog-specific categories drawn from TagNorm
(Agapito et al., 2021): vowel omission (`punta`→`pnta`), character elongation
(`sobra`→`sobraaaa`), abbreviation (`naman`→`nmn`), and code-switch-sensitive
distortions. The study covers **nine noise categories** and targets **15,000
annotated sentences** as the gold standard.

Splits, from `configs/base.py`: gold 80/10/10 train/val/test; synthetic 90/10
train/val with no test split, since final evaluation uses the gold test set.

## User Scenarios & Testing

### User Story 1 - Stage 1 synthetic pretraining (Priority: P1)

**Actor**: whoever runs `scripts/train.py`.

**Independent Test**: Run one short Stage 1 epoch on a handful of synthetic
pairs with a tiny T5 and confirm loss decreases and a checkpoint is written.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a clean corpus, **When** synthetic generation runs, **Then** it
   produces (noisy, clean) pairs with a precomputed `n*` for each.
2. **Given** Stage 1 data, **When** training runs, **Then** it uses the
   configured epochs and batch size for Stage 1 specifically, not Stage 2's.
3. **Given** any variant, **When** Stage 1 runs, **Then** the schedule,
   optimizer, and splits are identical across all three variants.

### User Story 2 - Stage 2 gold fine-tuning (Priority: P1)

**Status**: **BLOCKED** — the gold-standard dataset does not exist yet.

**Independent Test**: Substitute hand-written stand-in pairs and confirm the
Stage 2 code path runs with Stage 2's own epochs and batch size.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** gold data, **When** Stage 2 runs, **Then** it uses Stage 2's
   epochs and batch size, and resets best-validation tracking so Stage 1's
   best loss does not suppress Stage 2 checkpoints.

### User Story 3 - Skip a stage cleanly (Priority: P3)

**Actor**: a developer debugging with only one dataset available.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** no synthetic data, **When** training runs, **Then** Stage 1 is
   skipped with a logged message rather than crashing — necessary today,
   since only synthetic data exists.

### Edge Cases

- **Loss weights differ per variant**: ByT5 uses `L_CE` only; MrT5 adds rate
  and attention terms; TAHIMIK adds `L_NE`. One loss class handles all three
  via flags.
- **fp16 on CPU**: mixed precision is enabled only when CUDA is present.

## Requirements

- **FR-001**: Training MUST run in two stages, synthetic then gold, each with
  its own epoch count and batch size from config.
- **FR-002**: All three variants MUST use identical data, splits, optimizer,
  schedule, and seed — the control-variable requirement.
- **FR-003**: Synthetic generation MUST cover the manuscript's Tagalog-specific
  noise categories, not generic character corruption.
- **FR-004**: Every generated or loaded pair MUST carry a precomputed `n*`.
- **FR-005**: Best-validation tracking MUST reset between stages.
- **FR-006**: A stage MUST be skippable when its data is absent, with a log
  message and no crash.
- **FR-007**: The loss MUST activate only the terms relevant to the variant.
- **FR-008**: Checkpoints MUST record epoch, stage, and validation loss.

## Success Criteria

### Checkable now

- **SC-001**: A short Stage 1 run on synthetic data completes and writes a
  checkpoint.
- **SC-002**: Stage 1 and Stage 2 use their own configured epochs/batch sizes.
- **SC-003**: `best_val_loss` resets between stages.
- **SC-004**: Skipping a stage logs and does not crash.
- **SC-005**: Generated synthetic pairs carry `n*` values in `[0, 1]`.

### BLOCKED on the gold-standard dataset

- **SC-006** *(blocked)*: That Stage 2 improves over Stage 1 on real data.
- **SC-007** *(blocked)*: Reproduction of the Samuel and Straka style ERR gain.
- **SC-008** *(blocked)*: Inter-annotator agreement on the 15,000 gold
  sentences (`krippendorff` is already a dependency for this).
